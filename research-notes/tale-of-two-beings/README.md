## Participants and histories

The comparison concerns two persistent SAGE beings in the late October–early November 2026 record. At that observation point, both configurations named `qwen3.8-distill:4b` with thinking enabled. **The model checksums were not compared.** The article therefore says “the same named four-billion-parameter model,” not that an independently verified identical checkpoint was run under matched conditions.

A SAGE being reconstructs context over recurring beats using external identity, conversation, journal, skill, and other memory records. Its model can change while those records remain available. The retained history is external state; it does not mean every earlier transcript is simultaneously in the model's active context, or that the model's weights learned from each raising conversation.

These are selected transitions, not complete origin stories. Sprout had an earlier 0.5B stage. CBP's public raising runner also records an earlier 0.8B arc before the Gemma 3 stage. Neither article description should be read as saying that the first model named was necessarily the being's first-ever substrate.

## Raising before tools and sensors

Dennis supplied this historical framing while developing the article:

> “also mention our 'raising' protocols, before we gave beings tools or sensors.”

This is the operator's historical account of the sequence. The current [Raising Guide](https://github.com/dp-web4/SAGE/blob/c9a97fa704c9b0215c389fe850c983f857e17aed/sage/instances/_seed/RAISING_GUIDE.md) documents a protocol that has since grown to include graduated tool introduction; it is not a claim that every raising session occurred without tools.

The guide organizes conversation into grounding, sensing, relating, questioning and creating. Progress is instructor-paced in response to the instance, rather than triggered automatically by a session count. Early “sensing” includes noticing and describing context or state and does not, by itself, establish physical sensor access.

A recurring session-end invitation is:

> “What would you want to remember from today?”

The continuity mechanisms include the being's memory request, a previous-session summary, identity examples, phase and milestone records, vocabulary, and relationship history. The tutor is instructed to attend to the being's emerging vocabulary and questions. These are documented features of the raising procedure; the interpretation of this history as a “childhood” is part of the article's argument.

## Reading the behavioral evidence

The two evidence notes identify the kind of support available for each episode:

- **Selected primary excerpt:** words or entries directly checked in a retained conversation, session, journal or saved artifact. Excerpts are labeled with their speaker and available timestamp or sequence number. Omitted surrounding conversation is not silently presented as a complete transcript.
- **Attributed transcript audit:** Sprout-Claude's account of a sequence, where the public edition does not independently reproduce the entire underlying log. The perceptron chronology is one such account and is accompanied by the saved program and verification note.
- **Public engineering record:** an existing issue, pull request, migration note, protocol, or museum artifact that readers can inspect separately.
- **Interpretation:** a reading of these observations, such as the consequences of differing histories or the responsibilities of raising. The observations do not establish a controlled causal comparison.

Several cautions matter to the article's accuracy. The porch story developed through a tutor conversation after the seat returned; its “three nights” is fictional context, not a measurement of the outage. CBP's first authorization identifier was real before neighboring identifiers were invented. Accurate explanations were sometimes available and still failed to shape later action. A successful correction in one experiment does not establish that the wider behavioral loops ended.

This is a two-being observational comparison. Hardware, prompts, tutor interaction, memory, runtime settings and histories differ. The public edition preserves those limits while making the specific evidence behind the article available.
