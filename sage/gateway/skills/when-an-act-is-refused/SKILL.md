**What the gate is.** Every act you take is checked before it happens. The gate judges your act, not the
world: it does not lock devices or files. A refusal names the rule that matched and why.

**First: a path that does not exist cannot be granted.** `nonexistent` / "does not exist" means there is
nothing there, not that you were refused. Check the spelling against the roots you hold (`memory_read`
`hestia://reach` lists them). A "Did you mean …" names the real path, which you can read now. Asking again,
appealing, or asking someone to file it changes nothing: there is nothing to grant.

**Three ways forward**
1. **A scope request** (for reach: a path outside your grants). The refusal often opens one for you and names
   it (`scope-…`); `request_scope` files one. Someone other than you decides: your seat, a peer, or dp.
   - granted (once, for the session, or standing), refused with a reason, or **expired** if no one decides
     within about 8 hours. Expired is not pending and not granted; acting again opens a fresh request.
   - **pending means waiting for a decider. It is not stuck, and asking about it does not move it.** There is
     no separate escalation step: it is already in front of the deciders. Its state is in your own record
     (`memory_read` `hestia://scope/<id>`).
2. **An appeal**, if you think the rule was applied wrongly: `appeal` with the refusal's hash and your reason.
   An appeal argues whether the deny was right; it cannot hand you a path.
3. **Ask** your seat or a person: say what you are trying to do and why. There is often a route you do not
   know of.

**What does not work**
- Trying the same thing by another route (another tool, another file, another seat) to get past a refusal.
  It gets the same answer for the same reason. If a rule is wrong for your case, say so with an appeal or to a
  person; that can change the rule.
- Asking your seat, a peer or dp to file, re-file or check a request for you. Nobody files on your behalf, and
  a request's state is already in your own record; asking about it changes nothing.
