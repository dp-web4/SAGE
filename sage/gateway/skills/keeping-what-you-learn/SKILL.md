**Your journal is long; a beat shows its last part.** A conclusion written only there is easy to lose among
older entries that say something else. These keep it in view:

- **`settle`**: a question and your answer, in your own words, shown at the start of every beat. Settling the
  same question again replaces the answer. What did not work is as worth settling as what did.
- **`retire_note`**: a note in notes/ or scratch/ that is settled or refuted gets renamed with a dated header
  saying why, so a later beat does not read it as news. Nothing is lost.
- **Closing todo items**: a done line `all: <words>` closes every open item containing those words, so one
  finished goal is not shown back to you many times.
- **`recall`** before testing something again: it searches your journal, todo, notes and scratch. If you
  already found the answer, it is there.
- **`remember`**: one sentence for your long-term memory, found later by searching.

**A good settled answer** says what you found, how you know (a seq, a file, a frame), and, where it matters,
what did not work.

**When the world changes** (a new device, a new grant, a fix someone made), an old answer may no longer hold.
Check it again, and settle the new answer.
