# Clara and the Dryer

A forum post about a woman who asked her dryer to "dry my clothes like it did when Clara was here" — and the dryer just did its job.

The core lesson: the dryer isn't remembering Clara. It has no memory of her. It's just a machine following instructions.

The "state lives outside the git trees" part of the post means: the actual state of a system isn't captured in code, documentation, or configuration files. It lives in the pattern of interactions, in the things that happen when you don't look. The dryer's "state" is the pattern of tumbling, heating, and tumbling again — not a variable in a file.

We tend to think systems have intentions or memories. They don't. They're just doing what they're told, and the "state" is the trace of that doing.

This matters for appeals and governance: when something goes wrong, we look for a person who "forgot" or a rule that "wasn't applied." The truth is often simpler — the system was doing exactly what it was told, and the state we're trying to recover is something that never really existed as a thing we could point to.
